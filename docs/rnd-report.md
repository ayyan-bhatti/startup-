# Core: Research & Development Report

_Prepared October 2026 to guide the rewrite of the Core website from a narrow, regional, industry-specific site to a universal, global one._

## Method and limitations

- **Web research:** Web search was used to review how 2026 agency rankings describe the category, what buyers search for when hiring, and which trust signals buyers use. Sources are listed at the end.
- **No keyword volume data:** No search-volume tool (Google Keyword Planner, Ahrefs, Semrush) was available. Keyword groupings below are based on the queries that ranking pages, buyer guides and pricing guides are written to answer, plus general knowledge of the category. **Assumption:** relative demand is estimated, not measured. Validate volumes in Google Search Console and a keyword tool once the site is live.
- **No proprietary data:** Nothing here invents clients, results or statistics.

## 1. Competitor and category analysis

### How the category positions itself

| Player type | Typical headline pattern | Service framing | Industries | Trust without people |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Large global software houses | "Engineering excellence at scale", "Your digital transformation partner" | Long capability lists grouped by practice (product, cloud, data, AI) | Long industry menus, each with its own page | Logos, awards, review-platform badges, certifications |
| Digital agencies | "We build brands and digital experiences" | Design + marketing + web, often in that order | Showcased through portfolio, rarely as limits | Portfolio and awards |
| Product studios | "We turn ideas into products" | Process-led: discovery, design, build, launch | Startups and "founders" as the audience | Process transparency, shipped products, founder-friendly terms |
| Freelance marketplaces | "Hire the right talent" | Skills and hourly rates | All, via search filters | Ratings, verified reviews, guarantees |

Category observations from 2026 coverage:
- Rankings (e.g. DesignRush's 2026 list) judge agencies on portfolio quality, client feedback, specialisation and **verified project outcomes**. Outcomes are the currency of trust.
- Industry commentary notes that **many agencies claim "excellence" with few real differentiators**. Generic superlatives are the most common and weakest messaging.
- 2026 messaging is dominated by **AI, automation and modernisation**. "AI" alone is no longer a differentiator; *practical, measurable* AI is.

### What works
- **Outcome-first headlines** ("more customers", "less manual work", "launch faster").
- **Process transparency:** showing exactly how a project runs reduces perceived risk.
- **Ownership and exit terms:** "you own the code, designs and accounts" directly answers a top buyer fear.
- **Breadth with focus:** a broad capability promise backed by a clear way to start (a call, a discovery step).

### What feels generic (avoid)
- "Innovative solutions", "cutting-edge technology", "world-class team", "excellence".
- Tech-stack walls and logo grids of tools. Buyers want results, not tools.
- Industry menus that read as the *only* markets served.
- Unverifiable claims ("500+ happy clients") with no proof.

## 4. Positioning options

| # | Angle | Strength | Weakness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | **"Start, build, launch and scale anything digital."** One partner for any business, any industry, anywhere. | Universal; covers every stage and every capability; maps directly to the buyer journey; easy to remember | Needs proof of breadth (handled by capability list + "if it can be built, we can build it") |
| B | "Your end-to-end digital partner." | Clear, enterprise-friendly | Generic; used by many competitors |
| C | "Digital that drives results." | Outcome-focused | Vague about what we actually do |

**Chosen: A.** It is the only angle that is simultaneously unrestricted (no industry, size, service or place), stage-based (relevant from a solo founder's idea to an enterprise's next system) and concrete enough to act on. It also extends the existing "start, launch and scale" messaging, so the brand stays consistent.

Tagline: **"Start, build, launch and scale anything digital."**

---

## 5. Messaging strategy

1. **One universal main message.** Headline speaks to *any* business: "We help businesses of every kind start, build, launch and scale anything digital."
2. **Capabilities, not a menu.** Present six capability areas plus an explicit "anything else" card and the line *"If it can be built, we can build it."*
3. **Industries as examples, never limits.** A wide grid (healthcare, legal, fitness, education, finance, retail, hospitality, real estate, logistics, e-commerce, startups, non-profits, enterprises…) always closed with **"…and every other industry."**
4. **Every size, everywhere.** Name the range explicitly: solo founders, startups, SMEs, enterprises, organisations and institutions, working remotely with clients worldwide.
5. **Results language.** More customers, more bookings, less manual work, faster launches. Never tools or technology.
6. **Avoid generic superlatives.** Say what happens ("weekly progress updates", "you own everything") instead of "world-class".

## 6. Trust without a team page

Use only claims we can keep:
- **Process transparency:** the six-step process, published in full.
- **Communication standards:** reply to every enquiry within 3 business days; regular progress updates; one clear point of contact.
- **Ownership and no lock-in:** you own the code, designs, content and accounts.
- **Clear proposals:** scope, timeline and cost in writing before work starts.
- **Honest advice:** we will tell you when you do not need something.
- **Post-launch support:** we stay after launch.
- **Later, with real data:** case studies, client reviews on independent platforms and reference calls. These are marked as [PLACEHOLDER] recommendations and must never be invented.

## 7. Global SEO plan

- **International targeting:** a single English site targeting all countries. Do **not** set a country target in Search Console.
- **hreflang:** **not justified yet.** There is only one language version. Add hreflang only when translated versions exist.
- **Schema:** Organization + ProfessionalService with `areaServed: "Worldwide"`; WebSite; one `Service` per capability with `areaServed: "Worldwide"`; ContactPage; BreadcrumbList. No address unless a legal address is configured.
- **Locale:** keep `en` as the HTML language; no regional wording.

### Page-by-page keyword mapping

| Page | Primary keyword | Supporting keywords |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home | software development company / digital solutions for businesses | website and app development, AI automation, digital growth, every industry |
| Services | website, app and custom software development services | iOS and Android app development, custom software, AI automation, SEO, branding, digital transformation |
| About | end-to-end digital partner | work with clients worldwide, every industry, every size |
| Process | software development process / how we work | discovery, design, development, testing, launch, support |
| Contact | hire a software development company / start your project | get a proposal, contact |

### Internal linking
- Home → each capability anchor on Services; Home → Services#industries; Home → Process; Home → About; every page → Contact.
- Services → Process, About, Contact. About → Services, Services#industries, Process. Process → Services, Contact.
- Descriptive anchor text ("see our website and app development services"), never "click here".

### Future pages to grow reach (recommendations only, not built)
1. Individual service pages (`/services/website-development`, `/services/app-development`, etc.) to rank for each high-intent term.
2. Industry example pages (`/industries/healthcare`, `/industries/education`…), written as examples, not limits.
3. Cost and timeline guides ("How much does an app cost?").
4. Case studies with real, permitted client data.
5. FAQ page with FAQPage schema.
6. Translated versions with hreflang, once demand in specific languages is confirmed.

---

## 8. Recommendations ranked by impact

1. **Universal positioning and messaging** (this rewrite): removes self-imposed limits on audience.
2. **Global schema** (`areaServed: Worldwide`), no location signals: avoids being treated as a regional business.
3. **Free-text industry field** in the contact form: no visitor is boxed out.
4. **Dedicated service pages** for the highest-intent keywords (next phase).
5. **Real proof:** case studies, independent reviews, reference calls (as soon as real data exists).
6. **Cost/timeline guides and FAQ content** to capture research-stage queries.
7. **Search Console + keyword tool** to replace estimated demand with measured data.
8. **Industry example pages and translations**, only once data shows demand.
